PHASE 01 // ORBITAL RISK MISSION SAFETY

A fire alarm 80,000 miles from home

“Out there, between the Earth and the Moon, there is no back door. If something catches fire, you cannot step outside.” — Artemis II Mission Commander (Paraphrase)

On Earth, evacuation takes seconds. In cislunar transit and on the lunar surface, fire is an existential crew threat. Extinguishing it, scrubbing toxic hydrogen cyanide and carbon monoxide, and preserving the habitat atmosphere requires uncompromising materials screening before launch.

PHASE 02 // COMBUSTION PHYSICS FLUID MECHANICS

Flames behave differently without gravity

Earth (1.00 g)

Buoyancy-Driven Plume

Hot, light exhaust gases rise naturally. This drafts fresh ambient oxygen into the flame base, elongating the flame into an upward, flickering, hot orange teardrop.

Microgravity (0.00 g)

Diffusion-Controlled Sphere

No buoyancy means hot air does not rise. Oxygen reaches the reaction zone solely via molecular diffusion, forming a serene, low-temperature, spherical blue flame that can burn quietly and evade optical sensors.

PHASE 03 // EXPLORATION ATMOSPHERE HABITAT SPEC

Lunar habitats demand exploration atmospheres

To prevent decompression sickness (the bends) during frequent spacewalks, lunar surface habitats are planned to operate at 30% to 34% oxygen with reduced atmospheric pressure of ≤ 10.2 psi (70.3 kPa), compared to Earth's 21% O₂ at 14.7 psi.

METHODOLOGY & SCIENTIFIC LIMITS

Aerospace Screening Architecture

Mission Scope & Purpose

Ignis Atlas is an open-access screening architecture for spacecraft, transit vehicles, and lunar surface habitats (Artemis Program). It cross-references prospective cabin atmospheric baselines (O₂% concentration, total pressure, airflow ventilation velocity, and material flammability limits) against empirical microgravity combustion flight datasets archived in the NASA Physical Sciences Informatics (PSI) repository.

What We Do

We systematically index and benchmark empirical boundary envelopes from verified orbital investigations:

  • BASS & BASS-II: Solid fuel burning and extinction limits in the Microgravity Science Glovebox (MSG).
  • Saffire Series (I–VI): Large-scale fabric and solid composite combustion in expendable Cygnus resupply vehicles.
  • FLEX & FLEX-2: Isolated droplet burning, cool flames, and radiative extinction in the Combustion Integrated Rack (CIR).
  • ACME (CFI-G / CLD Flame): Gaseous spherical diffusion flames quantifying soot-free extinction limits in microgravity.
  • SoFIE (GEL / MIST / SMiFF): Standardized solid fuel ignition and extinction benchmarks for future spaceflight materials.

Strict Scientific Disclaimers & Operational Boundaries

1. Engineering Decision Support Only — Not NASA-STD-6001 Certification: Ignis Atlas is an early-stage screening aid. It does NOT constitute formal safety qualification or replace compliance certification under NASA-STD-6001 (Test 1 upward flame propagation, Test 2 toxic offgassing, or Test 7 downward smoldering) or formal Aerospace Flight Safety Reviews.

2. Deterministic Envelopes — Zero Hallucinated Probabilities: This system does NOT predict ignition likelihood, fire occurrence probability, or flame spread velocity. Flammability screening is deterministic based strictly on whether cabin parameters reside inside, near, or outside empirical database bounds.

3. The Partial-Gravity Void (0.16g Moon / 0.38g Mars): Querying the NASA PSI repository yields zero empirical reduced-gravity investigations. Natural buoyant convection does not cease on the Moon; rather, it transitions into an unmapped intermediate regime between orbital diffusion spheres and terrestrial buoyant plumes. All lunar predictions remain strictly categorized as Extrapolated or Data Gaps, highlighting the urgent mission need for the upcoming LUCI (Lunar Combustion Investigation) payload.

Data Attribution & Open Science

All research records, bounding coordinates, and hardware citations originate exclusively from public domain datasets in the NASA Physical Sciences Informatics (PSI) repository and corresponding peer-reviewed journals under CC0 / NASA Open Science directives.
